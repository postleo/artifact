Title: THE CARTOGRAPHER'S OATH
Author: (fictional demo screenplay)
Genre: Steampunk / Period Adventure
Hero Prop: THE MERIDIAN ASTROLABE — a fist-sized brass astrolabe-compass whose
concentric dials rotate to reveal hidden coastlines; scored with salt, warm to the
touch, one broken tine.

FADE IN:

EXT. PORT OF HALLOWTIDE — DOCKS — DAWN

Fog the color of tin. Gas lamps hiss on iron posts. Cranes groan over a forest of
masts. Steam ferries chuff at the quay, bleeding white into the grey.

SUPER: "Hallowtide. The last free port on the Drift."

A gull screams. Boots strike wet planking.

ISLA VENN (30s, oilskin coat, ink-stained gloves) moves fast through the crowd, a
leather map-tube slung across her back. She keeps one hand pressed to her breast
pocket, as if holding a heartbeat.

She ducks between two porters and stops at a NOTICE BOARD plastered with warrants.
One bill, freshly pasted, shows her own face beneath a single word: CARTOGRAPHER.

ISLA
(under her breath)
Already. That was quick.

She tears the bill down, folds it, pockets it.

EXT. HALLOWTIDE — ALLEY OF CLOCKS — CONTINUOUS

A crooked lane where every shopfront sells time. Isla slips into the shadow of an
awning and, finally, opens her breast pocket.

INSERT — THE MERIDIAN ASTROLABE

Cupped in her palm: a brass instrument, three nested dials, engraved with tiny
coastlines and a script no one alive reads. The outermost ring is bent — one tine
snapped clean. Where her thumb rests, the metal is worn mirror-bright.

BACK TO SCENE

She rotates the middle dial. It clicks — once, twice — and a hair-thin needle swings,
not toward north, but toward the sea wall.

ISLA (CONT'D)
(soft, to the astrolabe)
All right. West it is.

A SHADOW falls across her hands.

CADE (O.S.)
That's a hanging offense, you know. Holding a map to a place that isn't supposed to
exist.

Isla closes her fist around the astrolabe before she turns.

REVEAL: CADE MORROW (40s, revenue-officer's coat gone to seed, a mechanical brace on
his left hand that ticks faintly). He isn't reaching for a weapon. He's reaching for a
cigarette.

ISLA
It's a compass.

CADE
It's the Meridian Astrolabe. It points at the drowned city, and half the crown wants
it melted, and the other half wants it worse than that.

(beat)

I'm the half that wants a drink first.

INT. THE BRASS KETTLE — TAVERN — DAY

Low beams, copper pipes sweating condensation, a piano playing itself badly. Isla and
Cade take a corner booth. She sets the astrolabe on the table between them, keeps two
fingers on it.

CADE
May I?

ISLA
You may look. With your eyes. From there.

CADE
(leaning in)
The broken tine. You did that.

ISLA
My father did that. The night he gave it to me. He said a whole instrument tells you
where everything is. A broken one only tells you where you must go.

CADE
Poetic. Also useless in a court of law.

He produces the torn warrant — a copy of the one she pocketed — and lays it flat.

CADE (CONT'D)
They'll take your hands for this. Then they'll take the astrolabe and point it
themselves. You know what's out there. You know what happens if the wrong people
find the door.

ISLA
Then help me find it first.

A long silence. The piano hits a sour chord.

CADE
I get twenty percent of whatever's in the drowned vault.

ISLA
You get to keep breathing. That's the whole offer.

Cade grins, exhales smoke.

CADE
Sold.

EXT. HALLOWTIDE — SEA WALL — DUSK

The astrolabe's needle points dead at a section of ancient stone. Isla walks the wall,
counting flagstones, the instrument held out like a diviner's rod.

ISLA
Forty. Forty-one. Here.

She kneels, brushes salt from a worn medallion set in the stone — the same coastline
etched on the astrolabe's inner ring.

CADE
That's just a mason's mark.

ISLA
It's a keyhole.

She presses the astrolabe — bent tine first — into the medallion. It fits. She turns.

A deep, submarine GROAN. Water sheets away from a seam in the wall. A stair,
barnacled and black, descends into the dark below the tideline.

CADE
(quietly)
Every port has legends. I never once believed one.

ISLA
Bring a lamp. And Cade — if I say run, you don't ask which way.

INT. THE DROWNED STAIR — NIGHT

Lamp-light on wet stone. Their breath fogs. The astrolabe GLOWS faintly now, its dials
turning on their own, warm as a coal in Isla's hand.

CADE
It's moving by itself.

ISLA
It remembers the way. It's been here before. In someone else's pocket.

They descend. WATER drips in perfect, metronomic time. From somewhere below — a sound
like a great bell rung underwater.

CADE
Tell me that's the tide.

ISLA
That's not the tide.

INT. THE DROWNED VAULT — CONTINUOUS

A vast domed chamber, half-flooded, its ceiling studded with brass stars that once
were a working orrery. In the center, on a plinth ringed by black water: an EMPTY
CRADLE, shaped exactly like the astrolabe.

ISLA (CONT'D)
It's not a map to treasure.

CADE
Then what —

ISLA
It's a piece. The vault is the rest of the machine. Put the piece back and the whole
Drift can be charted. Every hidden port. Every smuggler's door. Every escape.

She looks at the cradle. Looks at the broken tine.

CADE
So put it back. Make your father right.

ISLA
If I put it back, it stops being broken. It stops telling me where I must go.

(beat)

It starts telling everyone.

Footsteps ECHO from the stair above — many boots, in step. Lamplight spills down.

REVENUE CAPTAIN (O.S.)
Miss Venn! In the name of the crown, set the instrument in its cradle and step into
the light!

Cade draws a battered pistol. Isla doesn't move. She stares at the empty cradle,
weighing the astrolabe in her palm.

CADE
(low)
Isla. Whatever the oath was — now's when you keep it or you don't.

ISLA
(quiet, decided)
My father's oath was never to finish the map.

She closes her fist. The glow dies. She turns the bent tine outward — toward the black
water, toward a channel no chart has ever shown.

ISLA (CONT'D)
Can you swim, Officer Morrow?

CADE
Badly.

ISLA
Perfect.

She grabs his sleeve. They plunge into the dark channel as the Captain's men flood the
vault behind them — reaching, too late, for an instrument already gone.

SMASH CUT TO:

EXT. OPEN SEA — DAWN

A skiff, stolen and small, rides a silver swell. Isla at the tiller, soaked, alive.
Cade bailing water and laughing like a man surprised to be breathing.

She opens her hand. The Meridian Astrolabe, salt-scored, one tine broken, points once
more not north — but onward.

ISLA
(to the astrolabe, a promise)
Where to next, then.

The needle swings. She smiles. She follows it.

FADE OUT.

THE END (of excerpt).
