Title: THE SALTGLASS RELIQUARY
Author: (fictional demo screenplay)
Genre: Fantasy / Period Drama
Hero Prop: THE SALTGLASS RELIQUARY — a hand-blown reliquary of cloudy sea-glass bound
in tarnished silver, holding a single drop of water that never evaporates; hinged like
a locket, hums faintly near the sea.

FADE IN:

EXT. ISLE OF COMB — CLIFF CHAPEL — STORM — NIGHT

Rain drives sideways. A chapel of grey stone clings to a cliff above a boiling sea.
Lightning strobes. Below, waves throw themselves at the rock and lose.

SUPER: "The Isle of Comb. Where the drowned are kept, not buried."

INT. CLIFF CHAPEL — RELIQUARY VAULT — CONTINUOUS

Candles gutter in the draft. Along the walls, hundreds of small SALTGLASS RELIQUARIES
rest in stone niches, each holding a single drop of sea. A congregation of grief.

MEREN TIDE (20s, novice keeper's robes, salt in her hair) moves down the row with a
taper, relighting candles the storm keeps killing.

She stops. One niche is empty. A reliquary is MISSING.

MEREN
(alarmed)
No. No, no —

She checks the ledger chained to the wall. Runs a finger down the names. Stops on one,
and her breath catches.

INSERT — THE LEDGER

A line in old ink: "TIDE, AREN — beloved father — kept the seventh spring."

BACK TO SCENE

MEREN (CONT'D)
(whisper)
They took my father's drop.

A heavy DOOR groans. FOOTSTEPS. Meren snuffs her taper and presses into the dark
between the niches.

REVEAL: PROCTOR VANE (50s, tax-collector's chain of office over a fine coat, dry as
bone in all this wet) enters with two GUARDS. In his gloved hand: a single reliquary,
its saltglass glowing faintly blue.

VANE
(examining it)
Remarkable. A drop of the sea that will not dry. Do you know what the mainland pays
for water that keeps its shape, keeper? For grief that can be bottled and sold?

He pockets Aren Tide's reliquary among a dozen others in a velvet case.

VANE (CONT'D)
(to the guards)
Take them all. The whole vault. Every drop.

Meren steps out of the dark before she can stop herself.

MEREN
That one is my father.

VANE
(unbothered)
Then your father is now crown property. As are you, if you raise your voice again.

The guards move to fill the velvet case. Meren watches her father's niche stand empty,
and something in her hardens like cooling glass.

EXT. ISLE OF COMB — SEA STAIR — DAWN

Grey light. The storm spent. Meren scrambles down a treacherous stair cut into the
cliff, following cart-tracks toward the harbor where a revenue ship rocks at anchor.

She reaches a tide pool and stops — because the water is HUMMING. She kneels. Cups it.

MEREN
(realizing)
You hum near the sea. All of you. That's why they have to move you inland — to make
you quiet. To make you keep.

She looks toward the ship, toward the velvet case aboard it, and understands: the
reliquaries will sing all the way to the mainland if she can only get close enough to
hear which one is his.

INT. REVENUE SHIP — HOLD — DAY

Meren, stowed away, soaked, moves between crates. The velvet case sits locked on a
table, guarded by ONE bored SAILOR.

She closes her eyes. Listens. Beneath the ship's creak — a chord of faint humming from
the case. And within the chord, one note she has heard her whole life: her father,
humming as he mended nets.

MEREN
(barely audible)
There you are.

She palms a fisherman's knife. The sailor yawns.

INT. REVENUE SHIP — HOLD — MOMENTS LATER

A scuffle, quick and desperate. Meren gets the case open. A dozen reliquaries glow
inside, all humming. She hovers her hand over them — and lets the one note guide her.

Her fingers close on a reliquary of cloudy saltglass bound in tarnished silver. It
WARMS. The single drop inside turns, catching the light like an eye opening.

INSERT — THE SALTGLASS RELIQUARY

Hinged like a locket. Silver worn smooth where a thumb has rubbed it for years. The
drop inside holds — impossibly — the faint shape of a man's face, mending a net.

BACK TO SCENE

VANE (O.S.)
Keepers are sentimental. It makes you predictable.

Vane fills the hold doorway, guards behind him.

VANE (CONT'D)
Put it back in the case, girl. One drop changes nothing. I have a thousand.

MEREN
(holding the reliquary to her ear, to her heart)
You have a thousand jars. I have my father.

VANE
And when I take him from you again?

MEREN
Then he goes back to the sea. Where he can hum as loud as he likes.

She turns and RUNS — up, toward the deck, toward the rail.

EXT. REVENUE SHIP — DECK — CONTINUOUS

Meren bursts into the light, guards a stride behind. Ahead: the rail, and beyond it the
grey and living sea. She skids to a stop at the edge, the reliquary clutched to her
chest.

VANE (O.S.)
Don't. That drop is worth a village. Throw it and you throw away a fortune.

MEREN
(over her shoulder)
It was never a fortune. It was a father you put in a jar.

She opens the reliquary's silver hinge. The single drop trembles on the lip of the
glass, humming, bright.

MEREN (CONT'D)
(softly, to the drop)
Go home. I'll listen for you from the shore. Every tide. I promise.

She tips the reliquary. The drop falls — a small silver line — and the instant it
touches the sea, the WHOLE OCEAN answers: a single, enormous, joyful note that shivers
the timbers of the ship and knocks the guards to their knees.

And then — quiet. Just waves. Just gulls.

Meren stands at the rail, the empty saltglass reliquary open in her hands, tears and
sea-spray indistinguishable on her face. She is smiling.

VANE
(shaken, for the first time)
...What have you done. They'll all want to go home now. Every drop. Every jar on the
mainland.

MEREN
(closing the empty reliquary, keeping it)
Good.

EXT. ISLE OF COMB — SHORE — DUSK

Meren stands where the surf meets the stones, the empty saltglass reliquary on a cord
at her throat. She closes her eyes and listens.

Beneath the waves — everywhere, and nowhere, and close as a memory — a man hums, mending
his nets, free.

She opens her eyes. Up and down the coast, other keepers are arriving at the water's
edge, empty reliquaries in their hands, come to listen too.

MEREN
(quietly, to the sea)
I'm here, Da. I'm listening.

The tide comes in. It hums.

FADE OUT.

THE END (of excerpt).
